## MARKET RESEARCH
For your reference only
 
**1. Nomadic Matt's Super Cheap Insider Guides** — NomadicMatt.com · $15-$20
- Why it sells: Highly trusted brand with decade-long authority in budget travel; destination-specific guides with day-by-day budget breakdowns, hostel recommendations, and free activity lists appeal directly to sub-$50/day travelers
- Gap: Guides can become outdated quickly as prices and visa rules change; lacks real-time updates and interactive features; limited coverage outside popular backpacker destinations
 
**2. Budget Travel Itinerary Templates & Spreadsheets** — Etsy · $5-$15
- Why it sells: Low price point removes purchase hesitation; editable Google Sheets and Excel templates for daily budget tracking, currency conversion, and expense categories resonate with cost-conscious planners who want control over every dollar
- Gap: Highly commoditized with hundreds of similar listings; no personalization or destination-specific data included; buyers often leave disappointed by lack of actual travel tips or insider knowledge
 
**3. The Points Guy / Budget Travel Hacking eBooks & Courses** — Gumroad / Teachable · $27-$47
- Why it sells: Teaches flight and accommodation hacking strategies like credit card points, mistake fares, and hostel loyalty programs that enable international travel well under $50/day; actionable ROI makes the price easy to justify
- Gap: Points and miles strategies are primarily US-centric and less applicable to non-American travelers; rapidly changing airline policies can make content obsolete within months
 
**4. Lonely Planet Digital Destination Guides** — Lonely Planet App / Google Play / Apple App Store · $10-$18
- Why it sells: Established global brand trust; offline access is critical for budget travelers avoiding expensive roaming data; covers budget accommodation, street food, and free attractions with mapped itineraries for over 150 countries
- Gap: Content updates are infrequent despite digital format; budget recommendations often skew slightly above the true backpacker price range; app interface criticized as clunky compared to newer competitors
 
**5. Budget Backpacker Masterclass: Travel the World for $40/Day** — Udemy · $12-$20 (on sale)
- Why it sells: Video-based learning appeals to younger traveler demographics; covers end-to-end budget strategy including cheap flights, couchsurfing, work exchanges, and food budgeting; Udemy's frequent 90% discount sales drive high volume purchases
- Gap: Quality varies significantly by instructor with little vetting; course content is often generic and not destination-specific; students report lack of community or ongoing support after purchase
